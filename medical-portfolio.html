01 / About

Comfortable moving across design, manufacturing, quality and the clinic floor.

[ portrait photo placeholder — 4:5 ]

I'm an MSc Medical Engineering (Design and Innovation) student at Keele University with a Materials Engineering background, and versatile, hands-on experience spanning the medtech product lifecycle — from design and prototyping through to regulated manufacturing, quality, and clinical device fabrication.

A device that passes design verification and a device that fits a patient on a Tuesday morning are two different problems — I've worked close enough to both to know they need the same rigour.

I'm proficient in SolidWorks and design control methodology — DfM, DfA, DfR, user requirements, weighted design criteria, concept generation and evaluation — with practical experience in device assembly, inspection, and technical documentation within ISO 13485-regulated environments. I combine direct patient-facing prosthetic and orthotic fabrication experience with statistical data analysis (SPSS) and working knowledge of ISO 14971 and ISO 10993.

I've also introduced process and documentation improvements in the roles I've worked in, and I'm comfortable moving across design, manufacturing, quality, and documentation functions rather than staying in one lane. I'm open to Design Engineering, Manufacturing Engineering, Quality/Regulatory, and R&D-support roles across the medtech ecosystem.

03 / Process

The design control pathway I apply to a new device brief.

1

Needs ID

Identify the clinical or user need driving the device brief.

2

User Requirements

Translate needs into user requirement specifications.

3

Concept Generation

Generate concepts against weighted design criteria.

4

Design & Evaluate

Evaluate concepts for DfM, DfA and DfR.

5

Prototype & Test

Build and test prototypes against requirements.

6

Transfer & QA

Support manufacture, inspection and quality release.
