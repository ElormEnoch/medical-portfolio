01 / About

Engineering that starts with how a person moves, not just how a device is built.

[ portrait photo placeholder — 4:5 ]

I'm a medical engineering graduate with hands-on experience across the full prosthetic and orthotic fabrication pathway — casting, rectification, composite lamination, and fitting. My interest sits at the point where biomechanics, materials science, and a patient's daily reality meet.

A socket that fits on paper and a socket that fits on a Tuesday morning walk to work are two different design problems.

Alongside device fabrication, I've spent time in clinical settings observing gait assessment and fitting sessions, which shapes how I think about tolerances, comfort, and iteration — not just structural performance. I'm equally comfortable in the workshop laying up carbon fibre as I am modelling a socket in CAD or reading a materials data sheet.

I'm currently looking for a role where I can keep building that range — deepening my fabrication craft while growing toward device design and clinical R&D.

03 / Process

The build pathway, from first cast to final fit.

1

Assess

Residual limb evaluation, gait observation, patient goals.

2

Cast

Capture shape via plaster cast or 3D scan.

3

Rectify

Modify the positive model for load and comfort zones.

4

Laminate

Layer and cure composite materials over the model.

5

Fit

Check socket trial, alignment, and pressure review.

6

Review

Follow-up adjustment based on wear and feedback.
